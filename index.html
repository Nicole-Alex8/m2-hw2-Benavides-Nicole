Why I Love Film

With everything being instant nowadays, I find it refreshing when something slows us down, especially when it comes to the art of photography. With film, you only have 36 photos to take. Instead of taking 10-15 photos of the same subject from different angles, film photography encourages us to slow down, prepare, and truly capture the moment.

There is also something so nostalgic about seeing the final results: the grain, the lighting, and the light flares. I genuinely get emotional every time I get a roll of film developed. There is something special about not knowing exactly how each photo will turn out until you finally get to see them.

Film Types

  • Color Negative: The most common type of film, produces an inverted color negative that is scanned or printed into positive photos. Prices can range from $10-$20
  • Black and White: Captures images in shades of gray. Prices can range from $6-$18
  • Slide/Transparency: Produces a positive image on a transparent film base. (I have personally never used this film before). Priced at ~$35 per roll of film

Film Format Types

  • 35mm: Most common format
  • Medium format (120 mm): Captures greater detail
  • Instant film: Self-developing film such as Instax and Polaroid

Tips

  • Know the type of photographer you are. Be honest: are you a beginner, intermediate, or advanced photographer?
  • Then, research the type of camera you want.
  • You don't need the most expensive film camera or film to produce a great photo.
  • Start with 24 exposures vs 36
  • Stick with one film stock and practice under different conditions.
  • Bring your camera with you everywhere you go; inspiration can strike anywhere.
  • If you have a special trip or celebration coming up, make sure you test-run the camera.
  • Have fun!
